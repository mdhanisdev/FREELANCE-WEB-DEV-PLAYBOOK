# CONTRACT & LEGAL [ NO CONTRACT, NO CODE ]
------------------------------------------------------------------------

## WHY YOU NEVER WORK WITHOUT A CONTRACT

------------------------------------------------------------------------

A contract is not a sign of distrust — it's the thing that lets both
sides relax. It says exactly what you'll deliver, what they'll pay, and
what happens when something goes wrong. Without it, every disagreement is
your word against theirs, and you have no leverage to get paid.

```text
✓ No signed contract + no cleared deposit = no work. No exceptions.
✓ A contract PROTECTS BOTH SIDES — say that to nervous clients.
✓ The contract's scope must match the proposal's scope exactly.
✓ Handshake deals are how freelancers get burned. Yours will be too.
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## THE CORE LEGAL PRINCIPLE

------------------------------------------------------------------------

```text
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  UNTIL YOU ARE FULLY PAID, THE WORK IS LEGALLY YOURS.            |
|                                                                  |
|  IP / ownership transfers to the client ONLY on FULL PAYMENT.   |
|  No final payment  →  no code handoff, no account transfer,     |
|  no license to use the work. This is your strongest leverage.   |
+------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

Everything else in the contract exists to support this one line. Put it
in writing and you rarely have to enforce it — clients pay because they
know the work isn't theirs until they do.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## WHAT THE CONTRACT MUST COVER

------------------------------------------------------------------------

```text
□ SCOPE OF WORK
    Exactly what you build — pull it from the signed scope doc.
    Anything not listed is out of scope (= a paid change request).

□ PRICE + PAYMENT SCHEDULE
    Total price and the split: deposit (30-50%) → milestone(s) →
    final before handoff. State currency and payment method.

□ TIMELINE + WHAT DELAYS IT
    Dates AND the triggers that move them. Explicitly:
    "Late or missing client content shifts the deadline accordingly."
    Timeline starts when deposit clears AND content is received.

□ REVISIONS
    How many rounds are INCLUDED (e.g. 2). State the cost of each
    EXTRA round. Define what "one round" means (consolidated feedback).

□ IP / OWNERSHIP TRANSFER
    Ownership of the final work transfers to the client ON FULL
    PAYMENT — not before. Third-party assets (fonts, stock, plugins)
    stay under their own licenses; name who buys them.

□ PORTFOLIO RIGHTS
    You keep the right to display the work in your portfolio and
    case studies, and to state you built it. (Add a quiet-period /
    NDA carve-out if the client needs secrecy.)

□ SUPPORT / MAINTENANCE
    Either the terms (what's covered, for how long, response time) OR
    a clear line: "Ongoing maintenance is NOT included; available as
    a separate agreement." Never leave this blank — it invites free work.

□ CANCELLATION / KILL FEE
    If the client cancels mid-project, you keep the deposit and are
    paid for work completed to date. Define the % or method.

□ LATE PAYMENT
    Interest or flat late fee after N days. Work PAUSES on overdue
    invoices. Access/handoff is withheld until paid.

□ CONFIDENTIALITY / NDA
    Mutual confidentiality of shared info. If the client provides a
    separate NDA, read it — make sure it doesn't kill your portfolio rights.
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## REVISIONS: THE MARGIN KILLER

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Unlimited revisions is how a profitable project becomes minimum wage.
Cap them in the contract and price the overflow.

```text
✓ Include a fixed number of rounds (2 is common). Write it down.
✓ Define a "round" = one consolidated set of feedback, not 15 emails.
✓ Extra rounds = a set fee per round, billed before you start them.
✓ Changes to the SCOPE (new pages/features) are change requests, not
  revisions — quote them separately at your normal rate.
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## SIGN THE CONTRACT + DEPOSIT INVOICE TOGETHER

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Don't separate the paperwork from the payment. Send them as one step so
there's no gap where work is expected but nothing is locked in.

```text
CLIENT ACCEPTS PROPOSAL
        │
        ▼
SEND CONTRACT (e-sign)  +  DEPOSIT INVOICE  (together)
        │
        ▼
CONTRACT SIGNED  +  DEPOSIT CLEARS
        │
        ▼
NOW — and only now — WORK BEGINS
```

```text
✓ Use e-signature so it's fast and legally logged (timestamps, IPs).
✓ Both parties sign. Keep a copy. The client keeps a copy.
✓ Deposit must CLEAR (not "sent") before you open the editor.
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## STAY OUT OF TROUBLE

------------------------------------------------------------------------

```text
✓ Use real names / legal entities for both parties — not nicknames.
✓ Match the contract scope to the proposal and scope doc, word for word.
✓ Never transfer code, repos, DNS, or account ownership before final pay.
✓ Keep every signed doc, invoice, and payment record in one folder.
✓ For HIGH-VALUE or unusual deals, have a LOCAL LAWYER review it — laws
  on IP, contracts, and tax differ by country. A template is a starting
  point, not legal advice.
```
