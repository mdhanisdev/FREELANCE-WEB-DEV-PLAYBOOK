------------------------------------------------------------------------

## PRICE THE SCOPE, NOT THE VIBE

------------------------------------------------------------------------

You already did discovery, so you're pricing a defined scope — not a
guess. That's the whole point of doing discovery first. A price without
a scope is a promise you can't keep.

```text
✓ Never quote a price before the scope doc is signed off in writing.
✓ Price the OUTCOME and the RISK, not just the hours.
✓ Uncertainty = higher buffer. Vague scope = you lose money.
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## THE THREE PRICING MODELS

------------------------------------------------------------------------

```text
HOURLY
  You bill per hour worked.
  Fits: ongoing work, unclear/changing scope, retainers, maintenance.
  Risk: client fears an open meter; you're punished for being fast.

FIXED-PRICE
  One agreed price for a defined deliverable.
  Fits: clear scope with a signed scope doc — the freelancer's default.
  Risk: scope creep eats your margin (that's why scope must be tight).
  → SAFEST for most freelance web projects.

VALUE-BASED
  Price tied to the value the site creates (revenue, leads, savings).
  Fits: clients where the site drives real money and they know it.
  Risk: hard to justify without data + trust; harder to sell early on.
```

```text
✓ Starting out? Use FIXED-PRICE with a tight scope. It's predictable
  for the client and protects you if — and only if — scope is locked.
✓ Use HOURLY for maintenance and "we're not sure yet" work.
✓ Grow into VALUE-BASED once you have results and case studies.
```

## HOW TO ESTIMATE (+ BUFFER)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Estimate in pieces, not one big number. Break the scope into tasks,
estimate each, sum, then buffer.

```text
□ List every deliverable from the scope doc (pages, features, integrations)
□ Estimate hours per item — be honest, use PAST projects as reference
□ Add setup/config: repo, hosting, CMS, deploy, DNS, testing, QA
□ Add COMMUNICATION time: calls, emails, revisions (people forget this)
□ Sum the hours
□ Add a BUFFER: 20-30% (more if scope is fuzzy or client is new)
□ Multiply by your effective rate → fixed price
```

```text
EXAMPLE (illustrative only)
  Build tasks ............ 40 h
  Setup / deploy / QA .... 10 h
  Comms + revisions ...... 10 h
  Subtotal ............... 60 h
  Buffer (25%) ........... 15 h
  Billable ............... 75 h  ×  your rate  =  fixed quote
```

```text
✓ The buffer is not padding — it's the cost of the unknowns that ALWAYS
  appear. Removing it doesn't make you cheaper, it makes you unpaid.
✓ Quote a RANGE only before scope is final; a single number after.
```

## PAYMENT STRUCTURE

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Never fund the client's project out of your own pocket. Split payment so
you're always paid ahead of the work delivered.

```text
DEPOSIT (30-50%)  →  MILESTONE(S)  →  FINAL (before handoff)
   upfront            at agreed         paid BEFORE you
   to start           checkpoints       transfer code/access
```

```text
✓ DO NOT start work before the deposit CLEARS in your account.
✓ For bigger projects, add milestone payments (e.g. design approved,
  build complete) so you're never carrying weeks of unpaid work.
✓ FINAL payment clears BEFORE you hand over source code, repo access,
  or transfer accounts. Until then the work is legally yours.
✓ Tie each payment to a deliverable, not a date, where you can.
```

## WRITING THE PROPOSAL

------------------------------------------------------------------------

The proposal turns your scope + price into a document the client says
"yes" to. Keep it clear and skimmable — no walls of text.

```text
□ SUMMARY        — their goal, in their words (shows you listened)
□ SCOPE          — pages, features, integrations (from the scope doc)
□ DELIVERABLES   — exactly what they receive (and what they don't)
□ PRICE          — fixed total + payment schedule (deposit/milestone/final)
□ TIMELINE       — phases + dates, with "starts on deposit + content"
□ ASSUMPTIONS    — client provides content by X; N rounds of revisions
□ TERMS          — validity of quote, what's out of scope, next step
□ CALL TO ACTION — how to accept (sign + pay deposit)
```

```text
✓ State clearly: extra revision rounds and out-of-scope work are billed
  separately. Set the expectation before the money, not after.
✓ Put an expiry on the quote ("valid 14 days") so it doesn't haunt you.
✓ The proposal's scope MUST match the contract's scope word for word.
```

## GETTING PAID ACROSS BORDERS

------------------------------------------------------------------------

If your client is in another country, plan the money BEFORE you send the
invoice — fees and delays surprise new freelancers.

```text
✓ Agree the CURRENCY up front (usually USD or EUR) and who eats fees.
✓ Use Wise or Payoneer for lower-fee international transfers vs raw bank
  wires; Stripe Invoicing works for cards in many countries.
✓ Factor exchange rate + platform fees into your price — don't absorb them.
✓ Confirm your country's rules for receiving foreign income / invoicing.
✓ Get the deposit through your chosen rail FIRST to prove it works.
```
